01

The Economic Value Chain

Every capital project sits somewhere on this ladder. Automation projects straddle the last two rungs.

Foundations
01

Primary

Raw materials

Extracts or harvests natural resources directly from the earth, water, or air.

  • Farming
  • Fishing
  • Forestry
  • Mining
  • Oil drilling

Provides the raw foundation for all other production.

02

Secondary

Manufacturing & processing

Transforms raw materials into finished or semi-finished products.

  • Steel mills
  • Car factories
  • Refineries
  • Construction

Adds physical value to raw materials — where OT lives.

03

Tertiary

Services & distribution

Provides intangible services and moves goods rather than making them.

  • Retail
  • Banking
  • Hospitals
  • Transport
  • Education

Connects manufactured products to the end consumer.

04

Quaternary

Information & knowledge

Intellectual, research, and technology activity that lifts every other sector.

  • Software
  • R&D
  • IT services
  • Policy
  • Consulting

Drives innovation and long-term efficiency across the chain.

The overlap that matters: in a processing plant, IT, OT, and AI form the digital nervous system — bridging the physical factory floor (secondary) with knowledge systems (quaternary). IT handles the data you type, OT handles the machines that move, AI optimizes both.

02

IT / OT / AI Convergence

Three systems, three priorities, one plant. Confusing them is how projects fail.

Integration

Operational Technology

Hardware and software that directly monitors and controls physical devices, valves, pumps, and machinery.

Keeps the physical production line safe, stable, and running 24/7.

  • SCADA — supervisory control and data acquisition across the plant floor.
  • PLC — rugged controllers wiring assembly lines and chemical valves.
  • DCS — distributed control across a large processing plant.

Information Technology

Enterprise systems that manage business data, communication, and digital transactions.

Manages commercial operations, compliance, logistics, and administration.

  • ERP — supply chain, inventory, and financial records.
  • CRM — sales and distribution scheduling.
  • Cloud & databases — secure storage for analytics and records.

Artificial Intelligence

Models and algorithms that ingest massive data streams to predict outcomes and act.

Finds hidden patterns to automate problem-solving and optimize efficiency.

  • Machine learning — forecast when a part will break.
  • Computer vision — scan passing products for microscopic defects.
  • Explainability — operators must see why an alert fired.
Comparison of OT, IT and AI priorities
DimensionOperational TechnologyInformation TechnologyArtificial Intelligence
Primary domain Physical plant floor — valves, motors, pumps. Virtual business network — servers, email, files. Algorithmic layer analyzing both domains.
Top priority Availability & safety a crash can cause physical damage. Confidentiality protecting business data from leaks. Optimization yield, energy, and error prediction.
Time horizon Real time — actions in milliseconds. Transactional — delays rarely cause physical harm. Continuous — live streams plus deep history.
Failure mode Unsafe process condition, trip, or asset damage. Data loss, breach, or commercial disruption. Silent drift, bias, or untrusted recommendations.

Worked example — refinery or chemical plant

OT

Reflex layer

Temperature sensors on a distillation tower detect a pressure spike and automatically adjust a valve to prevent a pipe rupture.

IT

Commercial layer

The system flags low raw chemical inventory, matches production rates against client invoices and market prices, and schedules a reorder.

AI

Predictive layer

Pulls vibration data from OT and maintenance logs from IT: "This compressor has an 87% chance of failing next Tuesday — schedule maintenance now."

03

Delivering the Digital Plant

Physical work is predictive. Software is adaptive. A single project has to be both.

Execution

Predictive (Waterfall)

Used for OT physical infrastructure.

Engineering→Procurement→Construction→Commissioning

You cannot undo a poured concrete foundation or a heavy reactor tank. Design is frozen before steel is cut.

Adaptive (Agile)

Used for IT software and AI models.

Sprint→Test→Retrain→Sprint

Short cycles write code, test algorithms, and update models against fresh data — without moving a single valve.

The four-phase integration lifecycle

1

Scope & Alignment

Define what the project solves without disrupting production.

PM focus: reconcile plant managers who care about uptime and safety with executives who care about ROI and data visibility.

Example: AI smart cameras for defect detection on an assembly line.

2

Architecture & Cyber-Safety

Design how IT, OT, and AI talk to each other securely.

PM focus: enforce the Purdue Model so a compromised corporate account cannot reach a physical valve.

Example: route sensor data through secure gateways before the AI cloud.

3

Build, Code & Train

Wire the physical plant while coding the software.

PM focus: manage a split timeline — IT trains models on history while OT pulls fiber and mounts sensors.

Example: simulation runs proving predictions match sensor readouts before go-live.

4

Commissioning & Go-Live

Turn the system on and make it part of daily operations.

PM focus: rigorous testing plus change management — operators must trust the tools.

Milestones: FAT in an isolated lab, then SAT on the live floor.

FAT

Factory Acceptance Testing

Integrated IT/OT system tested in an isolated lab environment at the integrator's shop — before anything ships.

SAT

Site Acceptance Testing

Every loop re-tested live on the plant floor during a scheduled, brief maintenance shutdown.

Critical trade-offs

RiskThe dangerMitigation
Scope creep "Cool features" added to an AI model slow delivery and blow the budget. Strict gatekeeping: a Change Control Board freezes software requirements before physical deployment.
Operational downtime Taking machines offline for sensor installs costs millions per day. Phased cut-overs: install only inside pre-planned maintenance windows.
The black box Operators ignore AI alerts they cannot explain. Explainable AI: requirements must demand the interface justifies every shutdown recommendation.
04

PAC&I Body of Knowledge

Process automation, control & instrumentation — four engineering tracks running in parallel.

ISA / PMI Baseline

Instrumentation, electrical, control systems, and networking move simultaneously. A PM who only understands one track cannot sequence the other three.

The PM's map. If it is not on a drawing, it is not in scope.

P&ID

Piping and Instrumentation Diagram

The definitive schematic of process piping, vessels, and control loops. The master scope document.

Instrument Index

Every tag on site

A database of every sensor, valve, transmitter, and actuator — tag numbers, vendors, ranges, locations.

Loop Diagrams

Wiring truth

How a field device connects to the junction box, marshaling panel, and PLC/DCS I/O card.

FDS

Functional Design Specification

The software blueprint: PLC logic, interlocks, and SCADA behavior under every operating scenario.

Functional safety is not a deliverable — it is a licence to operate.

ISA-84 / IEC 61511

Functional safety

The governing standard for Safety Instrumented Systems in the process industries.

SIL 1 – 4

Safety Integrity Level

High-SIL loops demand strict change management, independent validation, and audited procurement.

Hazardous Area

ATEX / NEC / Zone

Equipment for explosive atmospheres must be certified intrinsically safe or explosion-proof.

HAZOP & LOPA

Hazard review

Structured "what if" review of every node — including what happens if CV-101 fails open.

Segmentation is the control. Flat networks are the incident report.

Purdue Model

ISA-95 / IEC 62443

Segments the plant from Level 0 field devices up to Level 5 enterprise IT.

Field protocols

HART · Modbus

Understand bandwidth, determinism, and failure modes before specifying them.

Plant buses

Profibus / Profinet · EtherNet/IP

Topology, diagnostics, and segment limits drive cable and panel design.

OPC UA

The IT/OT bridge

Secure, structured data exchange between control systems and enterprise/AI platforms.

Estimating, sequencing, and testing are the three skills that keep a project solvent.

I/O Count Estimation

Cost engine

Panel size, server capacity, and programming hours all derive from I/O counts on the P&ID.

MTO Management

Material take-off

Cables, trays, conduits, tubing. A missed cable length stalls a crew and inflates cost.

Long-Lead Procurement

6–12 months

Control valves, analyzers, and DCS controllers must be specified in design, not construction.

Commissioning Sequence

Cold → hot → water → live

Cold loop checks, hot loop checks, water runs, then live process tuning. No shortcuts.

Brownfield Migration

Live plant cut-over

Replace legacy control while producing: hot cut-over procedures and constrained shutdown windows.

Requirements Traceability

P&ID to code

Every functional requirement maps from drawing to PLC block to HMI screen to test record.

Commissioning logic
Cold loop checks→ Hot loop checks→ Water runs→ Live tuning

Learn to read — and audaciously critique — P&IDs, loop drawings, and electrical schematics. Shadow senior instrument techs through a calibration turnaround.

ISA CCST path P&ID / loop reading
02

Months 6–18

System Integration & Execution

Own small PLC/SCADA upgrades and single-skid integrations. Live inside the FAT lifecycle until acceptance criteria are second nature.

PMI PMP ISA CAP ISA MT01
03

Months 18+

Safety & Enterprise Architecture

Lead multidisciplinary HAZOP reviews. Run brownfield migrations that merge legacy OT hardware with modern IT databases and predictive analytics.

TÜV / Exida FS Eng IEC 62443

Field experience beats coursework: volunteer to lead a FAT, sit through a hot commissioning window, and facilitate a P&ID review. That is where cross-discipline coordination is actually learned.

Vulnerability & mitigation

Financial

Commodity price flux

A project approved at $90/bbl can be uneconomic at $50/bbl mid-construction.

Control Economic stress-testing in design plus commodity hedging to protect near-term capex.
Financial

FX & inflationary spiral

Turbines and reactors sourced globally expose the budget to multi-currency swings and steel/nickel spikes.

Control Progressive indexation clauses and firm fixed-price commitments on critical equipment.
Supply chain

Package interface failure

Package A (platform) and Package B (pipeline) slip out of sync; one asset sits idle at massive daily cost.

Control Dedicated interface managers backed by legally binding cross-package penalty agreements.
Supply chain

Modular logistics bottleneck

Multi-ton modules built in low-cost yards and shipped to remote sites. One storm or canal closure halts installation.

Control Secure heavy-lift vessels early, build buffer storage at the destination port, schedule weather windows.
Technical

Subsurface uncertainty

Routing and anchoring face unknown geotechnical, seismic, or environmental hazards.

Control Front-end geological profiling, 3D seismic imaging, and explicit contingency for underground obstacles.
Technical

Labour & productivity deficit

Remote sites lack certified alloy welders and other scarce trades; turnover compounds delay.

Control Local vocational training, offsite modular fabrication, and premium camp conditions.
