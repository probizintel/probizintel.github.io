Programme Architecture

Fifteen courses, five stages. Each stage answers a question the next stage assumes you already answered.

Core

The sequence below is not the school's administrative order — it is the learning order. You cannot value a project (stage 2) before you can read a financial statement (stage 1), and you cannot argue strategy (stage 3) before you know your cost of capital.

Three editions, three ways of thinking

2008

PMBOK 4th

42 processes. Process and artefact driven — the era of “have you produced the artefact?”

2017

PMBOK 6th

49 processes, stakeholder management promoted, tool agility emphasised.

2021

PMBOK 7th

12 principles and 8 performance domains. The process list is gone — principles and outcomes instead.

Practice: use the 42-process matrix as a kick-off checklist, and the 8 performance domains of the 7th edition to judge whether the project created value or only documents.

03

The PMP Certification Track

The credential does not prove you can run a project. It proves you speak the same language as the people who hire you.

Credential
42%

People

Conflict, leading a team, negotiation, coaching, empathy. Most exam items are “what do you do next” scenarios.

50%

Process

Scope, schedule, budget, quality, risk. The heaviest slice — and the easiest to drill with practice questions.

8%

Business Environment

Compliance, organisational benefits, change. Few questions, easy to lose because it is skipped.

Weightings follow the current examination content outline; confirm on pmi.org before booking — PMI revises it periodically.

Study assets held in the library

A twelve-week plan

W1–2
The map

Read PMBOK once with no notes. The goal is to know what you don't know, not to understand everything.

W3–5
Scope, Schedule, Cost

The three heaviest areas. Do the arithmetic by hand — EVM, CPM, critical path.

W6–7
Quality, Resource, Communications

Mostly scenario questions. Drill with flash cards rather than re-reading.

W8–9
Risk & Procurement

The two areas engineers lose most points on. Memorise process order.

W10
First full mock

Full 180 questions, timed, closed book. The score matters less than knowing where you were wrong.

W11
Patch the gaps

Study only the areas below 60%. Do not re-study what is already right.

W12
Two final mocks

Three days apart. If both are above 75%, book the exam.

04

The Financial Appraisal Toolkit

Four calculations decide whether a project is approved. Run them here.

Interactive

This is the quantitative core of the project management and managerial finance coursework, moved out of a spreadsheet and into something you can use. Every formula sits next to its result so you can check it by hand.

Decision rule: accept if NPV > 0; accept if IRR > discount rate; shorter payback is better but is never the only criterion.

Level payment (annuity): the same amount each period, interest falling and principal rising. Every debt-funded project team should understand this before signing.

Depreciation is not cash flow, but it drives accounting profit and tax. Three methods give three different profit figures on the same asset.

The formulas underneath

NPV

NPV = Σ CFt / (1+r)t − CF0

Net present value: all future cash discounted to today, minus the investment.

IRR

0 = Σ CFt / (1+IRR)t − CF0

The rate at which NPV equals zero. No closed form — it is solved numerically.

WACC

WACC = (E/V)·Re + (D/V)·Rd·(1−T)

Weighted average cost of capital — the discount rate for a project of average risk.

Payback

t : Σ CFt ≥ CF0

Years to recover the outlay. Simple, intuitive, and blind to everything after the date.

Profitability index

PI = PV(inflows) / CF0

Useful under a capital constraint: rank projects by value created per unit invested.

Break-even

Q* = FC / (P − VC)

Minimum volume to avoid a loss. The denominator is contribution margin per unit.

05

The Reusable Research Framework

An MBA thesis proposal on risk management in oil & gas construction — personal identifiers removed, structure, method and risk taxonomy kept.

Method

On anonymity: author name, student number, advisor, company name and all identifying details have been removed. What remains is the method framework — the only part that transfers.

Proposal structure

1

Title — the pattern

“Risk management in oil and gas construction projects — a focus on offshore projects in Vietnam”

Formula: [topic] in [project type] — a focus on [specific scope] in [territory]. Three levels of narrowing in one line.

2

Background & overview

Market size, investment plans, number of live projects — with sourced figures. End on “however, identifying and assessing these risks remains difficult and few tools exist”.

3

Problem statement

Anchor in the triple constraint (scope, cost, schedule), then escalate to catastrophic consequence — industrial accidents as illustration, not as the subject.

4

Three research questions

  1. What are the key factors behind project risk? → identification and assessment
  2. Which can be avoided, and how? → avoidance planning
  3. If it materialises anyway, what minimises the effect? → contingency planning
5

Objectives

Establish the relationship between factors and project risk; measure the strength of that relationship; recommend an adequate risk management system.

6

Methodology

Qualitative as the main approach, quantitative for validation: questionnaires to owners, contractors and operators; semi-structured interviews with project managers; statistical analysis in SPSS; descriptive and explanatory design.

7

Scope & limitations

Limited to offshore projects over ten years. Concedes: large complex sample so results are not generalisable; risk management is more art than science, so the output is best practice, not a guaranteed system.

8

Literature base

Two standards compared: ISO 31000:2009 (governance frame, strong in finance and banking) and PMBOK 2008 (project processes, strong in energy and infrastructure). The thesis chooses PMBOK because the unit of analysis is a project.

The 59 ranked risk factors, in four bands

Drawn from prior research on oil and gas construction projects in Vietnam. Read it in bands: band 1 is largely inside your own organisation; bands 3 and 4 are things you can only prepare for.

01

Highest-ranked

  1. Bureaucratic government system and long approval procedure
  2. Poor design
  3. Incompetence of project team
  4. Inadequate tendering
  5. Late internal approval from the owner
  6. Inadequate project organisation structure
  7. Improper project feasibility study
  8. Inefficient and poor performance of constructors
  9. Improper project planning and budgeting
  10. Design changes
02

Second band

  1. Inadequate tendering price
  2. Delay in signing contract
  3. Inadequate coordination among contractors
  4. Ambiguous conditions of contract
  5. Lack of construction knowledge and experience
  6. Improper selection of project location
  7. Insufficient laws on projects
  8. Inefficiency of the legal process
  9. Increase of resettlement cost
  10. Constructability
03

Third band

  1. Exchange rate changes
  2. Changes of policies
  3. Increase of material cost
  4. Corruption and bribery
  5. Economic and financial crisis
  6. Increase of equipment cost
  7. Difference of standards and codes in JV
  8. Deference practice between foreign and domestic contractors
  9. Change in laws and regulations
  10. Inflation rate fluctuation
04

Fourth band & long tail

  1. Government interference
  2. Lack of coordination among contractors and subcontractors
  3. Work conditions differing from contract
  4. Poor quality of procured materials
  5. Ineffective supervision by consultants
  6. Poor relations and disputes with partners
  7. Unusual weather
  8. Inadequate type of contract
  9. Interest rate fluctuation
  10. Late material delivery

Plus a nineteen-item tail: material shortage, shortage of new technology, late lender approval, lender interference, inflexible lender rules, weak inter-agency coordination, environmental pressure, third-party damage…

How to reuse this: replace “offshore oil and gas” with your own sector, keep the eight-step structure, and re-run the questionnaire. The factor list will change — but band 1 is almost always internal capability and approval process.
